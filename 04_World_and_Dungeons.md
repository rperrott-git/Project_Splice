# World and Dungeons

## Confirmed preferences
The creator likes Jade Cocoon 2's dungeon-crawler structure and wants **replayability** to be substantial, primarily driven by collecting, breeding/splicing, optimizing and experimenting with monsters. Dungeons should have **recognizable elemental regions** but variable routes and encounters.

## Working world structure — DIRECTION
Central hub for archive/splicing, formation planning and preparation, linked to four elemental regions. The names and geography proposed earlier (Ashen Wilds, Flooded Ruins, Rootbound Depths, Storm Peaks) are placeholders, not canon. Each region features persistent landmarks and progression with randomized encounter pools, rewards and some route arrangements.

## Expedition types — PROPOSAL
- Egg-focused scouting: hunt variants and new species.
- DNA acquisition: target specific donors/passives/skills.
- Training: relevel recently spliced monsters.
- Elite hunts: difficulty spikes and exceptional specimen opportunities.
Could be **modifiers/objectives** for the same environments, not four separate game modes.

## Replayability devices — PROPOSALS
Handcrafted room library with shuffled routes, local modifiers, rare nests, special encounters, secrets, optional bosses, environmental effects, mixed-element variants, rotating rewards. Reward predictability matters: reduce excessive RNG stacking when pursuing particular builds.

## Persistent progression — DIRECTION
Major bosses/landmarks remain meaningful. Revisit earlier areas for new genetic opportunities. Don't erase investment in monsters through roguelike permadeath. Expeditions create enough resources and XP to support repeated level resets.

## Narrative relationship — DIRECTION
The AI directs protagonist toward guardians in elemental regions and uses their DNA for its eventual project. Exploration should include subtle consequences and environmental foreshadowing; specifics remain in `05_Story_SPOILERS.md`.

## Open
Top-down vs three-quarter traversal; region interconnectedness; exact procedural generation strategy; town size; enemy density and encounter trigger; extraction/capture mechanics; expedition loss penalties; checkpoints; elemental resistance system.

## Defeat, recall, and the in-world Failsafe — AGREED direction 2026-10-08
- The AI grants the child an early, knowingly accepted limited machine-to-human enhancement with **Save State** (biological reconstruction after defeat) and **Recall** (voluntary return to safety outside combat). This is distinct from the final guardian's full Human Splicing upgrade.
- A defeat in a dungeon triggers a return to the secured state/location through reconstruction, with **unsecured expedition rewards at risk**. Exactly which resources persist and what is lost remains to be balanced.
- Outside combat, Recall permits leaving an expedition **at any time**, securing collected rewards. Floor transitions should also prominently offer the choice to continue or leave.
- **Rare eggs and other unbanked expedition loot are at risk upon defeat** until safely returned. Voluntary retreat preserves earned rewards.
- The player's long-term monsters, already archived DNA and established progression are not permanently destroyed by defeat. Whether **newly captured** monsters count as unsecured loot remains OPEN.
- Saving and quitting the real game, especially on Android, must remain independent of in-world defeat mechanics: close/resume should not count as losing an expedition.
- **OPEN:** narrative and mechanical rules for how the Failsafe works after the AI turns against the protagonist; exact loss/retention rules, costs, and checkpoints.
